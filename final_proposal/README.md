# Final Project Proposal

For this final project, I want to pull data from the National Assessment of Educational Progress and tell a story about reading scores in the U.S. over the last thirty years. I looked at a couple of different options before settling on this one, including baseball stats, but I was more drawn to something with a variety of social stakes. I also liked that this data is all aggregated, so it's about groups of students and not individuals, which felt a lot better to me than some of the more personal public databases I've come across.
The main idea is that most people only see the average score go up or down, but that hides what's happening underneath. When you look at the whole distribution of scores, the lowest performing students have dropped a lot more than the highest performing ones, so the gap between them keeps stretching. I want to visualize that as a landscape, like a terrain of score distributions that changes year by year, with the top and bottom of the distribution marked so you watch one side slip while the other holds steady. I'm thinking about stretching a band between the two ends to show the tension of that gap growing.
I want to feature a few states to tell the story. I’m going to calculate which states have the most dramatic split between their top and bottom scores before I commit, so the choices are based on the data and not just my assumptions. I also want to layer in other factors like income, English language learner status, disability status, and per pupil funding, so viewers can pull the distribution apart and see who is actually in that bottom group. Technically, I'll pull the data once with a script, save it as static JSON, and load it. Since NAEP only tests every two years, I'll use lerp() to blend between them so the terrain flows. 
I expect a few challenges. NAEP only gives five percentile points per year, so any smooth curve is an estimate, and I'll have to make sure the visuals don't imply more precision than the data has. I think going through the data may take the most time.
I guess you could say this project is in response to last weeks homework on data. Ultimately, I think this final project will be a culmination of what I have learned and thought about this semester. Going through all the databases this past week, I was overwhelmed, so I'm looking forward to the opportunity for me to spend more time with it than just one week.
