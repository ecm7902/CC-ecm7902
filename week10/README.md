# MIDI visualizer

For my homework this week, I wanted to experiment with audio visualization that is more directly expressive of a song than just the beat or amplitude. In my undergrad, I did a music visualizer p5 project using fft and amplitude. Attaching a video of the demo here. I found that it was difficult to make this visualizer responsive to certain genres of music I put into it. It worked well for some songs and not others. More beat-driven songs with lots of changes in the volume looked cool but others didn’t look how the music made me feel. 
This time around I wanted to try something else. I tried using publicly available MIDI files since they have more to work with than flattened mp3 files. I also found a library of instrument samplers to work with tone.js so the midis didn’t just sound like synth sin sounds. Then, I mapped each note to a shape in the visualization. The note’s pitch determines its vertical position and color, its duration affects how wide the shape is, and its velocity affects its size. I used p5js for drawing the visuals and tonejs for playing and scheduling the notes. The song list and instrument mappings are separated in the song.js file. I tried to keep things modular by organizing into separate files.
