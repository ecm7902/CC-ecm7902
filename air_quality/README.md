For this week's project, I decided to pull data from the Open Meteo Database. I looked at a lot of different sources before I settled on this database. I sifted through public APIs and the government open data sites to try to find something more niche than weather data. I found some kind of interesting things, like a lot of information about taxes and financial histories, but I wasn't super excited about highlighting that in my project.

I also looked at some education and healthcare focused data sets, and these were a little more surprising to me. I found one that listed every single registered nurse in the state of Texas and had quite a bit of information about each nurse. It was searchable, so I searched for people I know who are registered nurses and was able to verify that it is up to date. I was surprised by that one because it just felt uncomfortable, and I know I would be uncomfortable having my information in a database on a government website like that. Similarly, there were some data sets on public education, focusing on financials, like the salaries of each superintendent of each school district, and comparing them with the schools' performances. That also made me uncomfortable, and I didn't feel great about working with that data set.

After looking at some weird things and reflecting on the types of things that are recorded and put on these public sites, I decided to just come back to the classic weather API. I focused on air quality and UV index. I picked three cities in different places, with different air quality and UV index norms, pulled the data for each of those, and made that into my visualization.

This week I focused on trying to modularize my code and keep as many things out of the draw function as possible, so I experimented with using constructors. I also wanted to work on making this responsive, so I tried to make the visualizer work at different screen scales. Overall, I was really trying to illustrate the movement going on in the atmosphere, creating a fluid and dynamic vibe visually to symbolize the data about the air. For the UV index, I was trying to symbolize brightness, so I created the illusion that you are turning your screen brightness up or down, the way the UV index measures the intensity of sunlight.
